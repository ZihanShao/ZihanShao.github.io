## About Me

I am a senior undergraduate student in **Mathematics at Nanjing University** and currently a visiting researcher at **Yale University**, where I work on LLM reasoning.

My research focuses on **LLM reasoning and interpretability**, particularly **latent reasoning, intermediate-state supervision and verification, and test-time computation**. I am interested in understanding how internal reasoning states evolve, when additional computation corresponds to meaningful reasoning progress, and how reasoning systems can become more controllable and verifiable.

Before working on latent reasoning, I studied **neuro-symbolic reasoning with description logic**, which led to a first-author oral paper at **ACM SIGKDD 2026**. More broadly, I am interested in connecting structured reasoning, internal representations, and learning-based reasoning systems.

## Selected Research

<div class="research-item">

### Recurrent Latent Reasoning

**Yale University · 2026–Present**

I am studying recurrent latent reasoning in pretrained causal language models. Instead of externalizing every intermediate reasoning step as text, the model iteratively updates a continuous latent workspace.

My current work focuses on how intermediate recurrent states should be supervised, how reasoning behavior changes across computation depths, and whether additional recurrent computation corresponds to genuine reasoning progress rather than redundant updates or premature convergence.

**Keywords:** latent reasoning · recurrent computation · intermediate-state supervision · verification · depth consistency

</div>

<div class="research-item">

### Differentiable Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning

**Nanjing University · 2024–2026**

I developed an operator-agnostic differentiable reasoning framework based on fuzzy **ALC description logic**. The framework translates expressive logical constraints into differentiable objectives and uses them to revise noisy neural predictions.

We systematically studied how different fuzzy operators affect reasoning and optimization behavior, and found that no single fuzzy semantics is universally optimal.

This work was accepted as a **first-author oral paper at ACM SIGKDD 2026**.

<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3817745">Paper</a>

</div>

<div class="research-item">

### Minimax Optimization for Vertical Federated Learning

**Nanjing University · 2026–Present**

I have also worked on minimax optimization for vertical federated learning, including block-coordinate methods, communication-efficient optimization, and error-feedback compression.

This project strengthened my background in mathematical optimization and iterative algorithms.

</div>
